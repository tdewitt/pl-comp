Lifecycle & Revocation Strategy — the Okta Question

Date 2026-07-16 Scope 29 active services, Secureframe TPRM register Headcount 78 people Question Does deprovisioning risk justify a dedicated IdP?
Bottom line up front

Don't buy Okta. One app of net-new spend (~$13.5k/yr) closes the real gap — you've already built the rest.

An IdP automates the full account lifecycle, and both directions matter: provisioning a joiner is recurring labor (the right access across every tool, by hand if unautomated), and deprovisioning a leaver is standing risk (a surviving account, session, or token). SSO login is the commodity part, and Google already does it. The question is whether automating grant-and-revoke across 29 apps justifies a dedicated IdP.

You've already answered it in production. 1Password runs Google Groups → SCIM bridge today: Google drives create/deactivate, no Okta involved. That is the recommended pattern, already live and proven. It leaves Slack as the one high-risk, all-staff app still lacking automated lifecycle.

The same lever handles both: SCIM, or a create/deactivate API. Mapping each app to that lever, 14 automate for $0 (IdP-native federation, or a free API below the SSO tier), and the rest gate automation behind a paid vendor SCIM tier or leave it manual. Okta does not remove that tax. The vendor charges it regardless of IdP, and Google can drive the same SCIM. Automating where the labor saved or risk removed clears the cost runs ~$1.1k/mo (Slack alone; 1Password is already done). Okta would be additive on top.

Two things matter: labor and risk

An IdP sells three things: SSO login, provisioning, and deprovisioning. Login is commodity (Google, free). The other two are the real work, and they pull in different currencies. Provisioning is recurring labor: every joiner and every internal move needs the right access across the right tools, granted by hand if nothing automates it. Deprovisioning is standing risk: every leaver's surviving account, live session, or personal token is exposure until the account is deactivated. Both are automated by the same lever, so the analysis scores each app once and weighs automating it by the labor it saves plus the risk it removes.

Why "has SSO" is not "lifecycle managed" — in either direction

Revoke: turning off someone's Google account blocks the login handshake but does not delete the downstream account, kill a live session, revoke a personal API token, or disable a local password. Grant: SSO/JIT at most creates a bare account on first login; it does not place the person in the right groups, roles, or projects. Both directions need SCIM or an admin API to actually manage the account. That lever is what this report scores.

Atlassian Guard — should you pay for it? No.

Guard does include SCIM deprovisioning (so it's not "just SSO"), but it is Atlassian-scoped only and bundles that lifecycle with enforced-SSO + org security policies at ~$4/user/month billed org-wide across every Atlassian product (~$372/mo here). Jira, Confluence, and Loom are all Low risk, and Atlassian's free managed accounts (domain verification) already let you centrally and manually deactivate a leaver across all three. Paying $4/user to automate deprovisioning of a wiki and issue tracker fails the risk test. Buy Guard only if you later want its security policies (session controls, token governance, audit) for their own sake — that's a hardening decision, not a lifecycle one.

What this costs

Three postures, priced

  • Recommended net-new: ~$1,125/mo (~$13.5k/yr) — Slack Business+, and possibly $0. 1Password (the other high-risk, all-staff, no-free-lever app) is already automated via the Google Groups SCIM bridge, at no incremental cost. Slack is the last one that clears on both axes (all-staff labor + high risk, no free lever). If Slack is already on Business+ (its enforced SSO suggests it may be), turning on SCIM is $0 too — confirm the current tier. Every other app is lifecycle-free-already (free create/deactivate API for both grant and revoke) or manual at low risk and low churn. GitLab and Vercel enforced-SAML are optional hardening; their lifecycle is already automatable via free API.
  • "SSO everywhere" strawman: ~$120k+/yr (≈$9.7k/mo of computable list prices plus 8 contact-sales/per-host unknowns). Buys enforced login you mostly already have via Google, and — critically — does not buy revocation on the apps that gate SCIM above the SSO tier.
  • Add Okta: strictly additive. ~$4–6/user/mo (~$4–6k/yr for ~80 users) on top of whichever vendor SCIM taxes you still pay. It removes none of them, because the vendors gate SCIM by their own tier regardless of IdP, and Google can drive that same SCIM.

Options & recommendation

Recommended

Google-as-IdP + targeted SCIM + scripted lifecycle

Buy SCIM only for Slack and 1Password (~$22k/yr). Script both provisioning and deprovisioning for the free-API apps. Manual + documented exceptions where risk and churn are both low. Drive it all from Google's provisioning catalog, which already covers the major apps and handles joiner automation as well as leaver.

Not now

Adopt Okta

Its pitch is exactly the joiner/mover/leaver automation that matters here — but it adds license cost and its own catalog nuances while removing zero vendor SCIM taxes, and Google already automates the same lifecycle. Genuinely wins at scale: when the volume of SCIM/script integrations, onboarding cadence, or access-review demand exceeds what the team can reliably operate by hand. Trigger-based, not today.
